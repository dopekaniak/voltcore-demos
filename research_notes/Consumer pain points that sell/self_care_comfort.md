# Self-Care, Wellness, and Home-Comfort Purchases (Candles, Bath, Blankets, Cozy Home Goods)

Scope: US consumer data, 2023-2026 preferred. Research date: 2026-11-04. Confidence labels: **[fetched]** = page opened and figure read directly; **[snippet]** = figure taken from a search-result summary, not opened; **[unverified]** = figure appeared only in secondary or unattributed text.

## Market size and growth: how big is the US candle / home-fragrance market?

### Takeaway
There is no single US figure. Estimates for the 2025 US candle market range from about $1.8bn to about $5bn depending on scope (manufacturing revenue vs. retail sales vs. scented/luxury sub-segments), and projected CAGRs range from roughly -4.5% (a single 2025 year, IBISWorld) to about +5.3%. The most-cited vendor figures disagree in both level and direction, so the report writer should present a range rather than one number.

### Cited Findings
- IBISWorld: US candle manufacturing revenue about $2.7bn in 2025, growth of -4.5% in 2025, and a 0.3% CAGR for 2020-2025 — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/market-size/candle-manufacturing-united-states/) **[snippet]** (direct fetch returned HTTP 405).
- IBISWorld: an earlier version of its page put 2025 revenue at $3.0bn; a 2024 snapshot showed a -3.6% CAGR for 2019-2024 — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/candle-manufacturing/4260/) **[snippet]**. Vendor-internal revision, not reconciled.
- Market Data Forecast: US candle market about $3.81bn in 2025, forecast CAGR 3.90% (2026-2034) — [Market Data Forecast](https://www.marketdataforecast.com/market-reports/united-states-candle-market) **[snippet]**.
- Market Research Future: US candles market about $1.84bn in 2025, CAGR 5.31% (2025-2035) — [Market Research Future](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/de/reports/us-candles-market-18177) **[snippet]**. Lowest estimate; narrower scope likely.
- Ken Research: US scented candles market valued at USD 2.4bn, growth driven by aromatherapy, natural products, and home decor — [Ken Research](https://kenresearch.com/us-scented-candles-market) **[snippet]**. Year not confirmed in snippet.

- Grand View Research (global): candle market to reach about $20.1bn by 2030, CAGR 6.4% (2025-2030) — [Grand View press release](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/candle-market-size-to-be-worth-20-09-billion-by-2030-grand-view-research-inc-301729008.html) **[snippet]**. Note: the release headline reads "$20.09 billion"; the summary reads "$20.10 billion". Minor conflict, flag.

- Circana: US prestige beauty grew about 2% to $16bn in H1 2025; Q4 2025 forecast of low-single-digit growth for prestige fragrance — [CEW / Circana](https://cew.org/beauty_news/circana-blog-unwrapping-fragrance-trends-and-what-to-expect-this-holiday-season/) **[snippet]**.

### Inferences
- The direction of the US candle market is uncertain: IBISWorld (manufacturing) shows decline, while forecast houses project low-to-mid single-digit growth. The disagreement is partly definitional (manufacturing revenue vs. retail/consumer spend).
- Circana's broader home and beauty data points to modest growth and a "joy at home" consumer posture, which is consistent with, but does not prove, growth in comfort categories.

### Gaps
- No Circana, NielsenIQ, or Mintel figure for US home-fragrance or candle retail sales in 2024-2025 was found or opened. Mintel's candle report page appeared in results but was not accessed.
- The Grand View US total candle market figure could not be found; only the luxury sub-segment was located.
- Bath, blanket, and cozy-home-goods market sizes were not searched for and are not covered.

## Conversion, repeat-purchase, and AOV benchmarks for candles, bath, and home-comfort products

### Takeaway
No category-specific (candle, bath, blanket) conversion, AOV, or repeat-rate benchmark was found from a primary source. The only primary-source benchmark retrieved is a general "good" repeat-purchase range (20-30%) from Klaviyo's glossary. Other numbers are from marketing blogs and are unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Klaviyo glossary defines repeat purchase rate as "the percentage of customers who have bought from you more than once" in a specific period, and says a good rate is "typically around 20% to 30%." It notes rates are higher for affordable or perishable goods and lower for high-value items (no numbers given) — [Klaviyo glossary](https://klaviyo.com/glossary/what-is-a-good-repeat-purchase-rate) **[fetched]**.
- Klaviyo 2026 Benchmark Report (secondhand summary): all-industries campaign open rate 31%, click rate 1.69%, placed-order rate 0.16% — source URL for the summary not confirmed; the Klaviyo report page was not opened **[unverified]**.
- Third-party analysis of about 167,000 Klaviyo brands: overall flow conversion around 1.42%, campaign conversion around 0.08% — [InboxAlly, Klaviyo Email Benchmarks](https://www.inboxally.com/blog/klaviyo-email-benchmarks) **[snippet]**; not opened; email-attributed, not site-wide conversion.
- Email benchmark blog: 90-day repeat purchase rate averages 15-20%, "good" at 25-35% — [ExcelOhunt US E-Commerce Email Benchmarks](https://excelohunt-site.pages.dev/blog/us-ecommerce-email-benchmarks) **[snippet]**. Low-authority domain; not opened.
- Aggregate figures attributed to "Shopify stores" (about 27% repeat rate) and an all-ecommerce average of about 28.2% (apparel ~20-25%) — no URL could be tied to these claims **[unverified]**. Candidate page: [TYB Shopify repeat-purchase article](https://www.tyb.xyz/blog/how-to-increase-repeat-purchase-rate-on-shopify-benchmarks-mechanics-and-what-actually-moves-the-number) (not confirmed as the source).

### Inferences
- Repeat-rate definitions differ (time window, minimum order count, customer-level vs. order-level). A 20-30% "good" range from Klaviyo and 15-35% ranges from blogs are not directly comparable to any single category.
- Replenishment-type products (candles, bath consumables, skincare-adjacent) plausibly sit at the higher end of repeat rates, per Klaviyo's own note on affordable or perishable goods, but no candle-specific figure supports this.

### Gaps
- No candle, bath, or home-comfort conversion rate, AOV, or repeat rate was found from a primary source (Shopify, Klaviyo category reports, or a trade association).
- Klaviyo's 2025 and 2026 Benchmark Reports exist, but their category-level repeat or conversion tables were not opened.
- Shopify's own commerce benchmark reports were not searched in this pass.

## Do comfort and self-care purchases rise in cold months, under stress, or after downturns?

### Takeaway
Surveys from 2025 consistently show that a majority of US consumers describe small treats or retail therapy as self-care and that stress or economic uncertainty pushes some of them to spend more. These are mostly brand- or lender-commissioned, self-reported surveys. No source was found that measures a cold-month or downturn effect on comfort-goods sales directly.

### Cited Findings
- LendingTree (fielded June 2-3, 2025, n=2,000 US adults 18-79, by QuestionPro): 54% consider retail therapy a form of self-care; 47% shop to improve mood; 38% say current financial stress has caused them to spend more. Among retail-therapy shoppers, personal care/beauty was bought by 42% — [LendingTree emotional shopping study](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/emotional-shopping/) **[snippet]**. The LendingTree page itself was not opened (a guessed URL returned 404).
- A separate LendingTree release (undated in results) reports 69% of Americans say emotions influence spending and 50% name stress as the top influencing mood — same source family **[snippet, date unclear]**; do not merge with the June 2025 wave.
- NCSolutions (April 2025, n=1,036 Americans): 62% consider small treats part of their self-care routine; reasons were impulse purchase (40%), coping with stress (34%), and self-care (30%) — [NCSolutions](https://ncsolutions.com/?p=62448) **[snippet]**.
- Cash App / Afterpay holiday report 2025: 71% of shoppers plan to buy self-care items this holiday season — [Cash App Afterpay Holiday Report 2025](https://cash.app/press/afterpay-holiday-report-2025) **[snippet]**; survey methodology not checked.
- Gen Z: 28% say they treat themselves to cope with stress; 21% as a reward for accomplishments — [Coupon Follow Treatonomics Index](https://couponfollow.com/research/the-treatonomics-index) **[snippet]**; date not confirmed.
- Zenoti: about 33% say self-care routines intensify during work-related burnout stress — no URL retrieved **[unverified]**.
- An undated survey reports Americans spend about $83 per month on self-care; relaxation is the top perceived benefit (79%), reduced stress next (66%) — no URL retrieved **[unverified]**.
- Capital One survey (cited by MoneyGeek): money matters are the top stress cause for 73% — [MoneyGeek, "Psychology of Treat Yourself"](https://moneygeek.com/financial-planning/psychology-of-treat-yourself) **[snippet]**; article last updated November 2024.
- U.S. Bancorp: 44% of adults spending more on self-care and wellness — date inconsistent in the snippet **[unverified]**; not used.
- Circana (July 2025): cost-conscious at-home consumers prioritize essentials and joyful at-home products — [Mammoth Times / Circana](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/gnwcq-2025-7-10-consumers-are-prioritizing-the-essentials-as-well-as-products-that-bring-joy-to-cost-conscious-at-home-living-reports-circana) **[snippet]**.

### Inferences
- Stress-linked and "treat yourself" spending is widely self-reported (roughly a third to a half of US respondents depending on question wording), which supports stress as a purchase trigger for small comfort items. Sponsor incentives (lenders, couponing, retailers) likely inflate the framing.
- The Circana "joy at home, essentials first" framing suggests comfort purchases are being retained during cost pressure, but this is an analyst observation, not a measured sales effect.

### Gaps
- No source found measuring cold-month lift in candle, blanket, or bath sales specifically (year-over-year or month-by-month).
- No primary post-recession or 2022-2025 downturn analysis of comfort-goods demand was found.
- Survey wording and sample definitions were not compared across the 2025 surveys.

## Seasonality: comfort/self-care sales vs. gift-driven Nov-Dec peaks

### Takeaway
Candles have a strong holiday/fall concentration. The National Candle Association's commonly repeated figure is that about 35% of candle sales occur in the Christmas/holiday season, though vendor-level estimates run from roughly 38-50% and up to about 60% for some. The seasonal peak for candles is therefore comparable to, or larger than, the general gift peak, but no source directly compares it with overall US gift-driven e-commerce seasonality.

### Cited Findings
- National Candle Association: about 35% of candle sales occur during the Christmas/holiday season; about 65% are non-seasonal — [NCA Facts & Figures](https://candles.org/facts-figures/) **[snippet]**; the NCA page is reported to cite a 2013 Mintel estimate for market size, so the figure is dated.
- Trade publication (undated): some vendors put holiday share closer to 60% **[unverified]**; source not opened.
- Vendor-level estimates: holiday is 38-40% of annual sales for some candle makers, with about half of that falling in Q4 — [Candle Science guide](https://www.candlescience.com/the-candle-maker-s-guide-to-a-successful-fall-and-holiday-season/) **[snippet]**; vendor marketing source, promotional.
- Same vendor guide: many small candle businesses generate up to 50% of annual sales from September through December; one brand reports up to 45% in its "candle season" (Q4) — same source **[snippet]**.
- Modern Retail (September 2023) on how candle companies prepare for their biggest season — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/this-is-our-showtime-how-candle-companies-prep-for-their-biggest-season/) **[snippet]**; no figures extracted.
- NPR (December 25, 2025) on tariff effects on holiday candle sales — [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5651286) **[snippet]**; no figures extracted.
- Research and Markets "State of the Industry: Candles in the U.S." reportedly breaks out holiday vs. non-seasonal sales for 2023-2029 — [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5440130/state-of-the-industry-candles-in-the-u-s) **[unverified]**; paywalled, not opened.
- A 2005 article described about one-third of annual candle sales falling in a two-month holiday window — [Vindy archive, 2005](https://vindyarchives.com/news/2005/dec/20/formerly-blazing-market-appears-to-be-smoldering) **[snippet]**; older data, shown for context only.

### Inferences
- For candles, the Q4 concentration (about 35-50% of annual sales across sources) is large enough that Nov-Dec gift demand and "treat yourself at home" demand overlap heavily; they cannot be separated with the data found.
- Vendor sources (Candle Science, trade press) have an incentive to emphasize seasonality; the NCA 35% figure is the most conservative and most-cited, and is the one to anchor on.

### Gaps
- No US gift e-commerce seasonality benchmark (share of annual sales in Nov-Dec for general gift categories) was found, so the comparison is not quantified.
- No monthly or weekly candle sales curve (e.g., Jan-Feb post-holiday dip, or spring/summer trough) was found.
- No source separates fall-winter "comfort" demand from holiday gifting for candles.
