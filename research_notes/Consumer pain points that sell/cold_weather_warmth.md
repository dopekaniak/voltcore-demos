# Cold-Weather Warmth Products as a Consumer Pain Point (Small Online Gift and Home-Goods Stores)

Research date: 2026-11-04. Search tool note: the Firecrawl search tool returned 401 errors for this session, so all searches ran through WebSearch (snippets only, with very few pages fetched in full). Treat every figure below as snippet-level unless marked otherwise. Limited tool budget (about 14 calls) meant that several questions remain open.

## Key Question 1: How large is the US market for blankets, throws, knitwear, and winter accessories, and what is its growth rate?

### Takeaway
No credible US-only figure for blankets and throws was found. Global throw-blanket estimates for 2025 range from about $2.7B to $7.5B, and the vendors disagree widely on both base year and scope. The best US-specific proxies are a North America share of about 38% and a Grand View US home-textile figure that includes blankets alongside other products.

### Cited Findings
- Global throw blankets, 2025 base: about US$7.48B in 2025 rising to US$8.88B in 2031, a 2.9% CAGR (LP Information, via MarketResearch.com) — [MarketResearch.com LP Information listing](https://www.marketresearch.com/LP-Information-Inc-v4134/Global-Throw-Blankets-Growth-42540277)
- Global throw blankets, 2025 base: US$2.68B in 2025, CAGR 5.71% for 2026–2034 (The Insight Partners) — [Insight Partners](https://www.theinsightpartners.com/reports/Throw-Blankets-Market)
- Global throw blankets, 2025 base: $3.2B in 2025, CAGR 5.2% through 2034; North America at 38.2% of global revenue, about $1.22B in 2025 (Market Intelligo) — [Market Intelligo](https://marketintelo.com/report/throw-blanket-market)
- Global throw blankets, 2025 base: USD 5.45B in 2025, CAGR 6.7% for 2026–2031 (Deep Market Insights) — [Deep Market Insights](https://deepmarketinsights.com/report/throw-blankets-market-research-report)
- US recycled-PET throw blankets only (a niche sub-segment): USD 420M in 2025, CAGR 9.6% for 2026–2034 (HTF Market Intelligence) — [HTF Market Intelligence](https://www.htfmarketintelligence.com/report/united-states-recycled-polyethylene-terephthalate-rpet-throw-blankets-market)
- Global blanket market (all blanket types, not throws only): USD 15.1B in 2023, projected USD 25.5B by 2030, CAGR 7.5% for 2024–2030 (Grand View Research blanket market page, as shown in search snippets) — [Grand View Research blanket market](https://grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/blanket-market)
- US home textiles overall (includes blankets, not blanket-only): USD 19.14B in 2023 rising to USD 26.69B by 2030, as reproduced by a reseller page citing Grand View Research. Not independently verified against Grand View's own page — [Reseller page reproducing Grand View figures](https://www.marketresearch.com/Grand-View-Research-v4060/Home-Textile-44621428/) (the search snippet came from this listing; the exact dollar figures could not be confirmed on the primary Grand View page)
- Grand View publishes a US Home Bedding report (2024–2030) segmented into Mattress, Bed Linen, Pillows, Blankets, and Mattress Toppers and Pads, and split by Online and Offline channel. The blankets-segment dollar figure is behind the paywall — [Grand View US Home Bedding](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-home-bedding-market-report/segmentation)
- Arizton publishes a US Electric Blankets report (2024–2029). No figures were extracted — [Arizton US electric blankets](https://www.arizton.com/market-reports/united-states-electric-blankets-market)

### Inferences
- Vendor disagreement is large: the 2025 global throw-blanket estimates span about 2.7x (USD 2.68B to USD 7.48B). Scope and definitions likely explain most of the gap, though this was not verified.
- Forecast CAGRs cluster roughly between 2.9% and 9.6%. The growth-rate range is probably the most reliable element to cite, with the caveat that it is vendor-supplied.
- Knitwear, scarves, hats, and winter accessories were not sized in any source found. No market-size figure for those categories is available from this research.

### Gaps
- No US-only size for blankets and throws was found from Grand View, Statista, IBISWorld, or Euromonitor. Statista and IBISWorld pages were not searched directly for this run.
- No US size for knitwear, scarves, hats, or winter accessories was found.
- Grand View's US Home Bedding blankets-segment dollar value was not accessible.
- Euromonitor was not searched.

## Key Question 2: What conversion rates, AOVs, and return rates are reported for winter and cold-weather products online?

### Takeaway
Winter-specific conversion and AOV data were not found. Home decor conversion benchmarks cluster around 1.2% to 1.9%, and the overall US apparel online return rate is widely cited near 24% to 26%, but the apparel figures come from a 2023 Coresight study and secondary sources, not from the NRF directly.

### Cited Findings
- Home decor ecommerce conversion: 1.4%, a figure often cited through Statsig's benchmark roundup (secondary attribution) — [Statsig ecommerce conversion by industry](https://statsig.com/perspectives/ecommerce-conversion-rate-industry)
- Home decor conversion: 1.9%, per ConvertCart's 2025 update — [ConvertCart](https://convertcart.com/blog/ecommerce-conversion-rate-by-industry)
- Home and furniture conversion: 1.24%, citing a November 2024 report (secondary, via InMotion Hosting) — [InMotion Hosting](https://www.inmotionhosting.com/blog/ecommerce-conversion-rates-by-industry.md)
- Overall ecommerce conversion reached 2.5% in Q3 2025, up 0.4% year over year (secondary source, citing an unnamed benchmark) — [Statsig](https://statsig.com/perspectives/ecommerce-conversion-rate-industry)
- Bedding and home textiles: Grips Intelligence reports revenue and traffic data for the category. Revenue split by device is Desktop 71%, Mobile 29%. No conversion rate was shown in the snippet — [Grips Intelligence bedding and home textiles](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/industries/home-garden/bedding-and-home-textiles)
- NRF and Happy Returns 2025 Retail Returns Landscape: total returns forecast at 15.8% of all purchases; online return rate forecast at 19.3% of online sales for 2025; projected dollar value about $849.9B, down from 16.9% in 2024 (as reported in the NRF 2025 press release coverage, verified only via snippets) — [NRF press release](https://cdn.nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025) and [Retail TouchPoints coverage](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/news/nrf-forecasts-nearly-850-billion-in-returns-in-2025-slight-decrease-from-2024)
- Online apparel return rate of 24.4%: Coresight Research 2023 study of US apparel brands and retailers. This is not an NRF figure. Labeled as older (2023) data — source cited through [Size.ly return statistics](https://www.size.ly/blog/ecommerce-return-statistics)
- One secondary source gives 26% of US consumers returned clothing bought online in 2025, attributed to Capital One Shopping. Not verified and treated as low confidence — [Opensend return statistics](https://opensend.com/post/return-refund-rate-ecommerce)

### Inferences
- A reasonable working range for a small home-goods store is conversion of about 1.2% to 2% (home decor), with overall ecommerce at roughly 2.5%. This is directional only.
- Apparel return rates (about 24% to 26%) are far higher than typical home-goods returns, but no home-textile return rate was found. Blankets and throws probably have lower return rates than knitwear, which is more size-sensitive. This is an inference, not a sourced finding.
- Winter products are likely to have AOVs below apparel but above accessories, but no AOV figure was found to support this.

### Gaps
- No conversion rate, AOV, or return rate was found specifically for blankets, throws, knitwear, scarves, hats, or winter accessories.
- No Shopify benchmark data for home textiles or winter apparel was found in this run.
- The NRF 2025 report's category breakdown (if any) was not reviewed.
- The 2026 NRF or Happy Returns return-rate figures were not checked.

## Key Question 3: How sharply do these sales spike with the first cold snaps, and how do they compare with the Nov–Dec gift window?

### Takeaway
Cold-snap spike data is mostly anecdotal and dated (UK retailers 2022, Minnesota retailers, a 2019 NBC account). The one large, recent figure is for gift spending: NRF 2025 holiday spending of about $890 per person, with roughly 70% of it for gifts. No measured link between first-freeze dates and product sales was found.

### Cited Findings
- Faribault Mill: bed blanket sales up about 75% year over year during a holiday cold snap, per a Star Tribune report on Minnesota blanket and parka makers. Snippet-level only; article not fetched — [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/who-won-last-weeks-arctic-blast-minnesotas-parka-and-blanket-makers/600239370)
- John Lewis (UK): fleece blanket sales up 386% and women's thermals up 146% in a 2022 cold-weather report — [Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2022/12/john-lewis-next-winter-sales/) (UK data, 2022, outside the US and date-window focus)
- An NPD analyst (2019, NBC) said a cold snap arriving after post-holiday markdowns had already started reduced its effect on full-price winter sales. Timing matters — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/freezing-temps-give-stores-january-thaw-flna1C8119013) (2019, older data)
- NRF 2025 holiday survey (Prosper Insights, 8,247 adults surveyed Oct 1–7, 2025): planned per-person holiday spending of $890.49, down 1.3% from $901.99 in 2024, the second-highest in the survey's 23-year history. Of the total, $627.93 is for gifts and $262.56 for seasonal items — [NRF press release](https://cdn.nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-to-spend-second-highest-amount-on-record-according-to-nrf-holiday-survey) (verified via search snippet; primary press release not fetched in full)
- The "snowblower effect" (consumers stocking up on winter gear after a cold snap reminds them of past misery) is described by analysts, with no named dataset — unverified, treat as anecdotal

### Inferences
- The gift window (Nov–Dec) is the largest annual demand event by dollar value, and it is driven by gifting rather than weather. Cold-snap spikes are short-lived and most likely overlap the gift window only in some years.
- A cold-snap trigger is plausible for blankets and warm accessories, but the magnitude of spikes is not sourced for US small stores. The reported 75% to 386% lifts come from different categories, years, and countries, and should not be generalized.

### Gaps
- No NOAA or regional first-freeze date data was linked to retail sales in this run. This remains unanswered.
- No Circana or NRF weekly sales-by-weather series for blankets was found.
- No current (2025–2026) US retailer report on cold-snap spikes was found. Several low-quality sites (accio-ai.com blog posts) cited "300%" and "38%" spike figures without sources; these were excluded.
- The Korean report citing lifts "as high as 530%" is from 2018 and was not verified; excluded from conclusions.

## Key Question 4: Do cold-weather products sell mainly to self-purchasers or as gifts?

### Takeaway
No product-level split for blankets, throws, or winter accessories was found. The NRF 2025 survey shows that holiday spending overall is gift-heavy, with about 70% of planned spend going to gifts and 95% of adult shoppers buying for family. Winter products are likely to share this gift-heavy skew in the Nov–Dec window, but this is not verified.

### Cited Findings
- NRF 2025 holiday survey: $627.93 of $890.49 per-person spend is for gifts (about 70%). NRF's own summary calls this "about two-thirds," which is a rounding difference — [NRF press release](https://cdn.nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-to-spend-second-highest-amount-on-record-according-to-nrf-holiday-survey)
- NRF 2025 survey: 95% of adults buy for family, 68% buy for friends, 34% buy for co-workers — same NRF press release source
- Survey sources disagree on the share of adults celebrating a winter holiday (91% vs 92.8%). Not resolved — [Walnut Invest holiday spending statistics](https://walnutinvest.com/stats/holiday-spending-statistics) as a secondary source

### Inferences
- Gift-heavy holiday spending suggests that blankets, throws, and knitwear are likely purchased as gifts in Nov–Dec, and as self-purchases during cold snaps. Both channels are plausible but neither split is sourced.
- Gift purchases in the holiday window may come with higher AOVs (bundles, gift sets) and with fewer returns, but no data supports this.

### Gaps
- No self-purchase vs gift split was found for blankets, throws, knitwear, or winter accessories specifically.
- No survey data on whether cold-weather purchases are self-purchased or gifted outside the holiday season.

## Source Quality and Conflicts Summary

- Market-size vendors conflict heavily on base year and scope. Global throw-blanket 2025 values range from USD 2.68B to USD 7.48B. Growth rates range from 2.9% to 9.6%. Treat all of these as unverified vendor estimates.
- Return-rate figures differ: NRF/Happy Returns online return rate of 19.3% (2025, all categories) vs Coresight 24.4% (2023, apparel) vs an unverified 26% (secondary). The apparel-specific rate is higher than the all-category rate.
- Home decor conversion ranges from 1.24% to 1.9% across sources (2024–2025 benchmarks).
- Grand View's US home-textile figure (USD 19.14B in 2023) comes from a reseller page and could not be confirmed on the primary Grand View page.
- The Star Tribune and Retail Gazette items are from news coverage. Star Tribune was not fetched in full; its figures are snippet-level.
- Low-quality blog sources (accio-ai.com) were excluded.
